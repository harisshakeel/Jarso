The reviewer came back with two open points. I'll settle one myself, and one is your call.

**My call: a deposit waiting for the parent holds that money in Spend.**

Without this, there's a failure path:

1. The child records "put in £5" while Spend has £5.
2. They then record a £3 purchase.
3. The parent has the £5 in hand but can't confirm it, because Spend now shows £2.

So waiting deposits get held the same way waiting withdrawals hold Save. Purchases and new deposits can only use Spend minus held money. The child sees "£5 waiting to go into Save".

**Your call: how much can a child undo a purchase?**

Right now the doc says any purchase, any time, with the parent not told. Undo exists for typos and double taps. Left wide open, though, a child could quietly undo last month's sweets and get their Spend back. A purchase that went over a limit could also drop out of the limit total.

My suggestion: undo only within 24 hours, and the parent sees "Undone" on that line in history, with no notification.

Does that work for you, or do you want it tighter (latest purchase only) or looser?

## Important

**I1. `evaluate_spend_limits` can undercount a window under same-child concurrency** (`20261029183422_purchases.sql:83`; the timestamp comes from :221 and :166).
- The window sum is capped with `occurred_at <= v_e.occurred_at`, and `occurred_at` is `now()`, which is the transaction start time, not the time the lock was acquired.
- Example: transaction A starts at t1 and B at t2 > t1, but B takes the advisory lock first. B commits with `occurred_at` = t2. A then runs and inserts with `occurred_at` = t1, so its sum excludes B's row. With a limit of 500 and two 300 purchases, neither sees a total of 600, so no notice is sent.
- A double-tap with two different keys is enough to trigger it.
- Fix: drop the upper bound in the sum, so the total is all live purchases from the window start onwards. The new row is always the latest committed state under the lock, so this is correct. Keep the bound only if the test seam needs it, for example by guarding it to `created_at`. Also add a pgTAP case where a later-inserted row has an earlier `occurred_at`.

**I2. `evaluate_spend_limits` counts notices with an unindexed scan of `notifications`** (`20261029183422_purchases.sql:102-107`).
- The query filters on `kind`, `source_type` and `source_id`, with no `child_id` or `recipient_id`. None of the four indexes serves that, so every purchase seq-scans `notifications`. That is harmless now and grows with the table.
- Cleanest fix: have `notify_parents` and `notify_child` return the number of rows inserted (`get diagnostics`, or `returning` into a count), and sum those in the loop. That also makes the result correct if the same window key is written twice.
- At minimum, add `and n.child_id = v_e.child_id`, which uses `notifications_child_id_idx`.
