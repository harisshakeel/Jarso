I read the slice 2 spec, the milestone spec, REQUIREMENTS FR01/FR03, architecture §5–§9 and the current migrations. No allowance, ledger or gift tables exist yet, `families.timezone` already exists, and there is no `clock_offset`. Four questions are still open, most important first.

1. **What does "Change amount or how often" do to the dates?**
   - D6 says a change applies from the next occurrence that hasn't been generated. That is enough for an amount change, but not for a frequency change.
   - Take a parent who moves weekly (Sundays from 2 Nov) to monthly. Which date is the first monthly occurrence? It could be the old `starts_on` stepped monthly, the day after the last generated period, or a new date the parent picks.
   - Can the parent also move the weekday or day of month without stopping and recreating the allowance?
   - Does this screen reuse the setup screen with its "First one on" field, or show only amount and chips?
   - Why it matters: if the date can change, the schedule needs a re-anchor date. Without one, every change keeps stepping from `starts_on`. The answer also decides the change screen and the period-key and next-date logic and its tests. A wrong guess means occurrences on dates the parent didn't expect.

2. **Can a parent ever clear a due allowance without handing money over?**
   - `closed` is unused, D5 says nothing expires, and S7/S8 say "already due stays due".
   - So after a stop, or a long gap, old occurrences stay due for good. The badge stays on, and slice 5 sends a reminder every 3 days, with no way to clear them except a fake handover of at least 1 minor unit.
   - Is that intended for the pilot, or should stop (or a per-occurrence action) offer "close without handover" using `closed`?
   - Why it matters: it adds or removes a parent function, a state change, audit and history copy ("not given"), and a balance invariant test. It is hard to add later once reminders are built.

3. **Can a first date or "Give on" date be in the past?**
   - If a back-dated `starts_on` is allowed, the first generator run creates every past period as a separate due occurrence. A yearly allowance started in 2024 would create several of them at once.
   - Why it matters: it decides Zod and DB validation (today or later only, or a limit on how far back), the generator's catch-up behaviour, and the summary-line copy. For gifts, a past `give_on` makes the gift due at once. That's harmless, but it should be decided together with this.

4. **When does a pause start, and is a period due on the pause day inside the pause?**
   - Does a pause always start the moment the parent taps "Pause", or can it be booked ahead ("pause over the holiday next month")? A booked pause needs a stored start date and changes how the pause window is modelled.
   - Does "already due before the pause" mean due by date (`due_on` before the pause started) or already generated? The generator runs hourly and can run late, so the two can differ.
