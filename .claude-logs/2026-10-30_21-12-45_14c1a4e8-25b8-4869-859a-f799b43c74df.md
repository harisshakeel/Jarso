The research agent has reported back. It didn't verify everything it set out to check (notes at the end). Here are the main findings and what they change:

**What the evidence says**
- **Put one next step first.** Duolingo rebuilt its home screen around a single guided path because learners didn't know which step to take next ([blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/)). This supports the "Do this next" card.
- **Show balances right under it.** Every kids' money app checked (GoHenry, Greenlight, Rooster Money, Revolut <18) is built around the balance.
- **Make the goal the child's own.** Rooster Money lets a child add a photo and a colour to a goal ([source](https://roostermoney.com/savings-pots-insights-2025/)). No study favours a ring, a bar or a road for this age group, so the plain bar is fine.
- **Streaks hurt kids.**
  - A 2026 study of children aged 6–12 using Duolingo found their goal turned into "keeping the streak", with "streak panic" at bedtime ([IDC 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3773077.3806127)).
  - The UK ICO children's code says children should be able to take breaks "without losing their progress" ([Std 5](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/5-detrimental-use-of-data/)).
- **Avoid:**
  - losing lives on a wrong answer (Greenlight);
  - points that turn into gift cards (Zogo);
  - goal confetti (Greenlight);
  - "don't miss out" wording.

**Changes I'd make to the mockup**
1. **Drop the Mon–Sun day blocks.** Grey empty days still read like a streak. Instead show progress that only goes up: "3 skills learned · 2 missions this week", with skill chips that stay earned for good.
2. **Goal gets an emoji or photo** the child picks, next to the name.
3. Everything else stays as it is: next step first, money second, plain bar with "£19 to go, about 5 more allowances".

**Not verified:** what sits at the top of each money app's home screen, and the apps BusyKid, Step, Acorns Early, Till, Monzo teen, Osper, Khan Academy Kids and Prodigy.

## 2. Gamified learning dashboards
- **Duolingo (2022 redesign)** turned the home screen into a single guided path. The stated reason: learners were unsure whether each step was "the best step". The next lesson leads and progress is the path itself. https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/
- **Duolingo on its own streak** says it works through "loss aversion". It also admits that breaking a streak "can feel quite demotivating" and that "fear of losing a streak could prevent learners from even attempting one". Streak Freeze was added as slack. https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/
- **Zogo** pays points ("pineapples") per module that convert to gift cards, and uses hearts. Reviews show learners chasing redemption thresholds rather than learning. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/zogo-learn-and-earn/id1474636588
- **Not checked:** Khan Academy Kids, Khanmigo and Prodigy.

## 3. Child safety and ethics
- **ICO Children's Code, Standard 5:** names "reward loops… notifications" as sticky features. It says to present options to continue "neutrally without suggesting that children will lose out", and to allow breaks "without losing their progress". Data-driven features that make disengaging hard are "likely to breach" the fairness principle. https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/5-detrimental-use-of-data/
- **ICO Standard 13 (nudges):** pro-wellbeing nudges are allowed. As children get older, design should move toward "supporting them in developing conscious decision making skills". https://www.privacylaws.com/media/3093/final-age-appropriate-design-code-of-practice.pdf
- **Kuo, Zhao & Scott, IDC 2026** (50 parent–child pairs, children aged 6–12, Duolingo): children's goals drifted to "keeping the streak". They reported "streak panic" at bedtime and guilt from the mascot. The authors recommend replacing loss-framed streaks with progress based on mastery, keeping badges through gaps, a pause that isn't framed as loss, and characters with "no disappointment scripts". https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3773077.3806127
- **5Rights, *Disrupted Childhood*** lists streaks as a persuasive-design harm. https://5rightsfoundation.com/resource/updated-report-disrupted-childhood-the-cost-of-persuasive-design/

## 4. Goal visualisation research
- The goal-gradient effect is solid: effort rises as people near a goal. A CMU field study measured hourly progress of 6.6%, 10.7% and 12.7% across the three thirds of a goal. https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/GoalGradBeh.pdf. Critics note the same effect can turn progress into obligation. https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/goal-gradient-effect-and-the-psychology-of-progress-bars-df6fd889fd8e
- **Not found:** any study comparing ring, bar and milestone road for children aged 11–14, and any study on "next step first" home screens for children. Duolingo's redesign is the only product evidence. Treat any shape choice as an untested design decision.

## Implications for the Jarso child Home
- **What leads:** one clear next step (today's mission or a pending inbox decision), as in Duolingo's path. Put the Spend and Save balances directly under it, because every money app above is built around the balance.
- **The goal:** make it the child's own, with a name and a photo or emoji (Rooster Money). Show amount saved out of target plus "N payments left" as plain counting, without a percentage pressure line. The shape is open since no evidence favours one. A bar or the jar filling is easiest to read. If the goal is complete or paused, show it in a calm, neutral way.
- **Learning progress:** show cumulative mastery (skills learned, badges kept forever), never a count of consecutive days. Pausing loses nothing (ICO Standard 5, IDC 2026 D2 and D4). The mascot never sounds sad.
- **Avoid:**
  - lives or hearts and losing them on a wrong answer (Greenlight)
  - points that convert to money or gift cards (Zogo; it also clashes with "never reward amount")
  - confetti on reaching a goal (Greenlight)
  - "don't lose out" copy on prompts to continue
  - notification loops driven by guilt
